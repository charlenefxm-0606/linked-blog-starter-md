## 1. 🌀 Topspin — The Windshield Wiper Technique

**Goal:** Generate reliable topspin using a low-to-high brushing motion and complete follow-through.

### What Zach Noticed About My Technique

I sometimes prepare my paddle too late.

By the time the ball approaches, I haven't fully positioned my paddle or body for the shot. This affects my timing and makes it harder to generate controlled topspin.

Another issue is that I sometimes let the ball get too close to my body and paddle, leaving insufficient room for a comfortable swing.

### My Topspin Technique

**Step 1 — Prepare Early**

- As soon as I recognize the incoming ball, begin preparing my paddle.
    
- Don't wait until the ball is already close.
    
- Get into an athletic position with bent knees.
    
- Use a slightly staggered stance, with my left foot ahead of my right when appropriate for my forehand.
    
- Face generally toward my target while allowing natural body rotation.
    

**Step 2 — Create Space**

- Watch the ball carefully.
    
- Track where the ball is going to bounce.
    
- Adjust my feet to create enough space between my body and the incoming ball.
    
- Take a small adjustment step backward when I'm crowded, but avoid backing up automatically on every shot.
    
- Allow the ball to reach a comfortable contact point.
    

**Step 3 — Brush Low to High**

- Begin with my paddle below the ball.
    
- Keep the paddle face relatively neutral or slightly closed.
    
- Swing forward and upward.
    
- Brush up the back of the ball to generate topspin.
    
- Use the windshield-wiper feeling that helped me during class.
    

**Step 4 — Follow Through**

- Continue swinging forward toward my target while brushing upward.
    
- Don't stop the paddle immediately after contact.
    
- Maintain enough forward movement to send the ball over the net.
    
- Increase swing speed gradually while keeping control.
    

### 🌟 My Breakthrough

By the end of class, I was able to generate topspin consistently during the drill!

The technique that clicked for me was:

**Paddle slightly closed → Low-to-high swing → Windshield-wiper brushing motion → Complete follow-through.**

**Important distinction:** Brushing creates spin, but the shot still needs forward momentum. If I brush too vertically without moving through the ball, my shot may fall short.

⭐ **Personal Cue: "Prepare early. Make space. Brush up and through."**

## 2. ⏱️ Early Preparation & Timing

**Goal:** Stop being late to the ball and improve contact consistency.

A recurring observation from today's class was that I sometimes start getting ready only after the ball is already approaching.

### What I Need to Change

- Recognize the incoming shot earlier.
    
- Prepare my paddle as soon as I know which shot I'm playing.
    
- Move my feet to get into a comfortable position.
    
- Keep my knees bent.
    
- Give myself enough room to contact the ball in front of my body.
    
- Avoid unnecessary last-second movements.
    

**Key Reminder:** I often have more time to prepare than I realize, especially on slower balls or shots that bounce.

For fast volleys, preparation needs to be compact and immediate.

⭐ **Personal Cue: "See it early. Prepare early."**

## 3. 🌸 Kitchen Dinking — Less Movement, More Control

**Goal:** Keep dinks low, controlled, and consistent.

During kitchen practice, Zach emphasized keeping my paddle movement simple.

### Key Reminders

- Prepare the paddle before the ball reaches me.
    
- Keep the paddle in front of my body.
    
- Use a relaxed grip.
    
- Avoid excessive wrist and hand movements.
    
- Maintain a stable paddle face.
    
- Use a small, controlled movement to guide the ball.
    
- Let the ball come to my paddle rather than reaching unnecessarily.
    

### What I Should Avoid

- Flicking my wrist excessively.
    
- Making a large backswing.
    
- Changing paddle angles at the last second.
    
- Overhitting a ball that only needs a gentle touch.
    

⭐ **Personal Cue: "Quiet hands. Soft grip. Small movement."**

## 4. 🛡️ Backhand Blocking & Paddle Position

**Goal:** Develop a more dependable backhand block that stays low.

We practiced using the backhand to block or redirect incoming shots.

### Paddle Position

- Keep my paddle comfortably in front of my body.
    
- Prepare the backhand face before contact.
    
- Maintain a stable paddle angle.
    
- Avoid excessive tilting or wrist movement.
    
- Use minimal backswing.
    

### How to Return the Ball

For a regular backhand block:

- Let the ball meet the paddle.
    
- Use a short, controlled movement.
    
- Keep the paddle face directed toward the intended target.
    

For a more aggressive backhand volley:

- Add a small forward punch or push.
    
- Keep the paddle stable.
    
- Aim the ball into open court or toward an opponent's feet when appropriate.
    

**Important:** A straight or neutral paddle face is a helpful starting reference, but the exact angle depends on the ball's height, speed, and desired trajectory. Don't force the face perfectly vertical for every shot.

⭐ **Personal Cue: "Paddle in front. Stable face. Small push."**

## 5. 🔥 Blocking vs. Resetting vs. Attacking

**Goal:** Recognize when to absorb the ball and when to add forward pressure.

One important lesson was that not every incoming ball should be treated the same way.

### 🧊 Reset — Take Pace Off the Ball

**Use when:** I'm under pressure, defending, or trying to neutralize a fast shot.

- Relax the grip.
    
- Keep a stable paddle face.
    
- Minimize extra paddle movement.
    
- Absorb incoming pace.
    
- Aim for a soft landing in the opponent's kitchen.
    

**Reminder: Don't actively punch the ball when I'm trying to reset it.**

### 🛡️ Block — Control the Incoming Shot

**Use when:** An opponent hits hard and I need to react quickly.

- Paddle in front.
    
- Compact movement.
    
- Stable wrist and paddle face.
    
- Redirect the ball safely.
    

A block can be firm or soft depending on the situation.

### 🔥 Attack — Apply Pressure

**Use when:** The incoming ball is high enough or otherwise attackable.

- Move the paddle forward with a short, controlled punch.
    
- Keep the paddle face stable.
    
- Direct the ball into a difficult area.
    
- Aim down toward the court when contact height allows.
    
- Avoid excessive swings.
    

**Key Difference:**

|Shot|Main Intention|Paddle Action|
|---|---|---|
|Reset|Neutralize pressure|Softly absorb|
|Block|Control incoming pace|Compact, stable contact|
|Attack|Apply pressure|Short forward punch or controlled swing|

⭐ **Personal Cue: "Defending? Soften. Attacking? Drive forward."**

## 6. 🎯 Attacking Opponents' Feet

**Goal:** Recognize when opponents are vulnerable and choose effective targets.

I learned a useful tip from another player:

When an opponent is positioned away from the kitchen line, their feet may become an effective target.

### Why This Works

- Low balls can be difficult to volley upward.
    
- Opponents may have to hit while moving.
    
- It can force defensive returns or pop-ups.
    
- It makes attacking more about placement than raw power.
    

### How to Apply It

- Observe whether opponents are at the kitchen line or farther back.
    
- If they are off the line, look for opportunities to send a controlled ball toward their feet.
    
- Keep the ball low.
    
- Use topspin when appropriate to help the ball dip.
    

**Important:** Seeing an opponent's shoes isn't a perfect indication of their positioning. Focus on their actual location and whether the incoming ball gives me a safe attacking opportunity.

⭐ **Personal Cue: "See open feet? Aim low."**

## 7. ❌ Doubles Strategy — Remember the X

**Goal:** Understand court responsibilities and reduce confusion over who takes each shot.

Today we discussed the idea of an imaginary **X** connecting players across the court.

This helps illustrate diagonal relationships and which player may be better positioned to cover certain shots.

### What I Learned

- Pay attention to which opponent is hitting.
    
- Recognize diagonal shot paths.
    
- Understand my responsibility within the doubles formation.
    
- Anticipate where the next ball might travel.
    
- Communicate when the ball is approaching the middle.
    

### Important Clarification

The X is a useful court-awareness reminder, not a fixed rule that the diagonal player must always take the ball.

Who covers a shot depends on:

- Ball trajectory and speed.
    
- Each player's position.
    
- Forehand or backhand availability.
    
- Whether one player has been pulled out of position.
    
- Communication between partners.
    

### Middle Balls

When the ball travels between partners, communicate clearly.

Useful calls:

- **"Mine!"**
    
- **"Yours!"**
    
- **"I got it!"**
    

Don't assume your partner knows what you're thinking.

⭐ **Personal Cue: "Read the X. Cover your space. Communicate."**

## 8. 💃 Dance With Your Partner — Court Positioning

**Goal:** Move together and maintain effective spacing in doubles.

Zach described doubles positioning as dancing with your partner.

### What This Means

- Move laterally together when the ball moves across the court.
    
- Maintain a connected formation.
    
- Avoid leaving a large gap between partners.
    
- Don't crowd each other.
    
- Adjust as the opponents move the ball.
    

### The Paddle-Touch Distance

One useful visual from class:

When both players extend their paddles toward each other, they should be relatively close to touching.

This is a practical spacing reminder, especially when both players are at the kitchen line.

It isn't an exact measurement for every situation. You still need enough space to swing safely and cover your assigned area.

### What I Should Practice

- Sliding sideways with my partner.
    
- Maintaining similar depth when appropriate.
    
- Covering the middle without overcommitting.
    
- Adjusting together after angled shots.
    
- Recovering into position after each exchange.
    

⭐ **Personal Cue: "Move together. Stay connected."**
