**Focus:** Third-Shot Drops, Returns, Blocking, Footwork & Mental Game

## 1. Third-Shot Drop & Soft Returns

**Goal:** Develop soft, controlled shots that land in the opponent's kitchen.

### Two-Person Practice

- One player stands near the kitchen line; the other stays at the baseline.
- The kitchen player feeds balls to the baseline player.
- The baseline player practices returning the ball softly into the opposite kitchen.

### Technique

- Avoid swinging aggressively.
- Use a gentle **low-to-high pushing motion**.
- Imagine softly tossing a beanbag over the net.
- Keep the paddle movement small and controlled.
- Aim for the ball to land in the opponent's kitchen.

**Key reminder:** Soft shots require touch and repetition, not power.

_Note: This drill primarily develops third-shot-drop technique from the baseline, rather than returning an opponent's third-shot drop._

## 2. Returning Serves & Hard Shots

**Goal:** Control the ball's direction, speed, and depth while maintaining balance.

### Technique

- **Stay low:** Bend the knees and maintain an athletic stance.
- **Control the paddle angle:** An overly open paddle face can send the ball too high.
- **Watch the timing:** Avoid contacting the ball too early, too late, or from an awkward position.
- **Avoid overswinging:** Use a smooth, controlled stroke rather than excessive force.
- **Stabilize before hitting:** Avoid swinging while still running forward.

### Footwork & Timing

- Move toward the ball.
- Take small adjustment steps.
- Plant or stabilize your feet briefly before contact when possible.
- Swing while balanced, then continue moving.

**Key reminder:** Move → Balance → Hit.

**Important distinction:** Staying low improves balance and positioning, but it does not automatically make the ball travel slower or lower. Paddle angle, swing path, and contact point also determine the trajectory.

## 3. Mental Game — Develop a "Monk Mode" 🧘‍♀️

**Goal:** Stay calm, focused, and emotionally steady regardless of the score or opponent.

### Accept Mistakes

- Everyone makes mistakes, including professional players.
- A missed shot is information, not a personal failure.
- Avoid criticizing yourself after every error.
- Focus on the next point instead of replaying the previous mistake.

### Stay Calm Under Pressure

- Don't panic when a fast ball approaches.
- Keep your eyes on the ball.
- Trust your preparation and react naturally.
- Avoid rushing your movements.

### Don't Absorb Other Players' Emotions

- Aggressive or competitive opponents may create pressure.
- Their intensity does not need to become yours.
- Maintain your own pace and playing style.
- Focus on what you can control rather than how others behave.

### Focus on Improvement, Not the Score

- A game ending 11–0 or 11–1 does not define your ability.
- Every game is an opportunity to practice a specific skill.
- Losing one game does not predict the outcome of the next.
- Measure progress by improvement, not just wins.

### My Match Reset Routine

After a mistake:

**Exhale → Relax → Reset → Next ball.**

**Key reminder:** I cannot control every point, but I can control how I respond to it.

## 4. Blocking — Fire & Ice Drill 🔥🧊

**Goal:** Learn to absorb powerful shots and return them softly into the opponent's kitchen.

### Two-Person Practice

- **Fire:** One player hits fast, aggressive shots.
- **Ice:** The other player stands near the kitchen line and practices soft blocks.

### Blocking Technique

- Keep the paddle in front of the body.
- Stay low and balanced.
- Use the backhand for balls that naturally come to that side, especially near the middle of the body.
- Angle the paddle slightly to direct the ball over the net.
- Avoid a large backswing or forceful follow-through.
- Let the incoming ball's pace provide much of the energy.

### The Most Important Adjustment

**BLOCK — DON'T PUSH.**

Sometimes a movement that feels like a gentle push is actually adding too much force.

For soft blocks, the paddle should remain relatively quiet.

Think:

**Stable paddle → Soft hands → Absorb the pace.**

### Mental Focus

- Don't become intimidated by powerful shots.
- Watch the ball rather than reacting to the opponent's aggressive movement.
- Keep your body relaxed enough to respond.

**Key reminder:** A hard incoming ball doesn't always require a hard response.

_Note: A firmer block may be appropriate when defending against certain attacks. The goal of this drill is specifically to develop soft blocking and resets._

## 5. Footwork & Court Positioning

**Goal:** Move efficiently, maintain balance, and avoid unnecessary injuries.

### Safe Movement

- Use controlled lateral steps and lunges to reach balls.
- Avoid excessive backward walking or backpedaling.
- Turn and move safely when chasing deeper balls.
- Don't sacrifice balance or safety for an unreachable shot.
- Warm up dynamically before playing.

**Key reminder:** Staying balanced is more important than chasing every ball.

### Transitioning to the Kitchen

- After hitting a third-shot drop, look for an opportunity to move forward.
- Advance when the shot gives you enough time.
- Avoid rushing forward if the opponent can attack.
- If caught in the transition zone, use a controlled reset to create another opportunity to advance.

### Avoid Getting Stuck in "No Man's Land"

The transition zone is the area between the baseline and kitchen line.

- It can be a vulnerable position when opponents attack.
- However, it is also a necessary part of moving forward.
- Learn to defend and reset from this area rather than rushing through it without control.

**Key reminder:** Advance when possible. Stabilize when necessary.

### Kitchen-Line Positioning

- Maintain a balanced, athletic ready position.
- Avoid unnecessary foot movement when the ball is coming directly toward you.
- Use small adjustment steps when needed.
- Be aware of your position relative to the kitchen line.

### Important Kitchen Rule

You cannot volley the ball while touching the kitchen or its line.

You also cannot allow your momentum from a volley to carry you into the kitchen, even after hitting the ball.

However, **you are allowed to enter the kitchen to hit a ball that has bounced**.

**Key reminder:** Stay balanced behind the kitchen line when volleying, but don't become afraid to move your feet.

## 6. Backhand Development

**Goal:** Build confidence and consistency using the backhand during rallies.

### Practice Focus

- Use the backhand when it is the natural shot for the incoming ball.
- Avoid constantly maneuvering around the ball to force a forehand.
- Keep the paddle in front and minimize unnecessary movement.
- Practice backhand blocks, volleys, and controlled returns.
- Gradually become comfortable using the backhand under pressure.

### Match Practice

Choose one specific backhand goal per session.

For example:

**"Today, I will use my backhand naturally instead of avoiding it."**

Don't worry about winning every point while developing the skill.

**Key reminder:** Confidence comes from repetition, not avoidance.

---

## 7. Structured Practice — One Skill at a Time

**Goal:** Improve systematically without becoming overwhelmed.

### Practice Principles

- **Focus on one skill at a time:** Avoid trying to correct multiple techniques simultaneously.
- **Set a clear intention:** Decide what specific skill or movement you want to improve before practicing.
- **Prioritize consistency:** Develop control and repeatability before increasing speed or power.
- **Use repetition:** Give yourself enough opportunities to become comfortable with a movement.
- **Introduce pressure gradually:** Start with relaxed drills before applying skills in competitive situations.
- **Avoid constant adjustments:** Don't rebuild your technique after a few mistakes.
- **Reflect on progress:** Pay attention to what feels more natural, controlled, and consistent over time.

### Personal Practice Mindset

- Every game can be an opportunity to work on something specific.
- Improvement doesn't have to be measured by the final score.
- Different skills develop at different speeds.
- Focus on building a reliable foundation rather than mastering everything immediately.

**Key reminder:** Have a clear purpose when practicing, but don't turn every practice session into a performance test.
