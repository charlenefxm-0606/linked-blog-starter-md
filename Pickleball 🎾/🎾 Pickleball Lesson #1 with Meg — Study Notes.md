## 1. Serving — Build a Simple, Repeatable Motion

**Goal:** Develop one reliable serve that feels natural and becomes automatic through practice.

### Drop Serve Technique

- **Stance:** Use a semi-open stance rather than facing completely forward.
- **Body movement:** Use the legs and gentle body rotation to generate power.
- **Stay low:** Keep knees bent and body leaning slightly forward throughout the swing.
- **Eye contact:** Watch the ball and keep the head steady until completing the follow-through.
- **Paddle angle:** Maintain a consistent paddle face to control the ball's trajectory.
- **Follow-through:** Swing smoothly toward the intended target without stopping abruptly.

**Key reminder:** Avoid standing up, leaning backward, or adding unnecessary movements during the serve. These can disrupt timing and consistency.

### Drop Serve vs. Bowling Serve

- **Drop serve:** Allows a natural groundstroke-like motion but introduces an additional variable: the ball's bounce.
- **Bowling serve:** A simpler, underhand motion that may be easier to repeat under pressure.
- Neither technique is universally better. Choose based on personal comfort, consistency, and natural movement.

**Meg's advice:** When practicing, focus on improving technique. During league games, simplify the motion and use whichever serve is most reliable.

**Priority: Get the serve IN before worrying about power or placement.**

### Mental Approach to Serving

- Avoid overthinking mechanics during matches.
- Don't change techniques after a few missed serves.
- Use one simple cue instead of trying to remember multiple instructions.
- Recognize that nervousness and inconsistency are normal parts of learning.
- Practice under low pressure before gradually introducing match-like situations.

**Practice analytically. Play simply.**

## 2. Third-Shot Drop & Getting to the Kitchen

**Goal:** Transition safely from the baseline toward the kitchen line.

- Practice hitting third-shot drops while a partner is positioned at the kitchen.
- Use a soft, controlled shot that lands in the opponent's kitchen.
- Focus on control and placement rather than power.
- Move forward when the shot gives you enough time to advance.
- Avoid rushing toward the kitchen when the opponent can attack the ball.

**Key reminder:** The third-shot drop helps create an opportunity to move forward; it doesn't mean you must reach the kitchen immediately.

---

## 3. Blocking & Soft Hands

**Goal:** Absorb incoming pace and return the ball with control.

- Stay low with knees bent.
- Keep the paddle in front of the body.
- Avoid large backswings or unnecessary arm movements.
- Use soft hands to control the ball rather than swinging aggressively.
- Imagine gently tossing an egg or beanbag over the net.

**Key reminder:** Let the paddle do less work. A controlled block often requires very little movement.

---

## 4. Footwork & Backhand

**Goal:** Improve court coverage while maintaining balance.

- Move sideways or take small adjustment steps to reach incoming balls.
- Use a controlled lunge when necessary.
- Avoid excessive twisting or reaching from awkward positions.
- Become more comfortable using the backhand instead of constantly repositioning to hit a forehand.

**Key reminder:** Move the feet to create a comfortable hitting position rather than forcing an awkward swing.

---

## 5. Ready Position & Split Step

**Goal:** React more quickly and maintain balance.

- Keep knees slightly bent in an athletic stance.
- Hold the paddle in front of the body.
- Use a small hop or split step timed around the opponent's contact.
- Land balanced and ready to move in either direction.

**Key reminder:** Be ready before the ball reaches you.

---

## 6. Cooperative Rallying & Consistency

**Goal:** Develop touch, control, patience, and repeatable movements.

### 100-Ball Rally Drill

- Rally cooperatively with a partner.
- Use gentle, controlled shots.
- Prioritize keeping the ball in play rather than hitting winners.
- Gradually work toward **100 consecutive successful shots**.
- Focus on relaxed movements and consistent contact.

**Key lesson:** Not every ball needs to be attacked. Consistency and patience are valuable skills.

## 7. Mental Game — Stay Calm Under Pressure 🧠

### Challenges Identified

- Becoming nervous or self-conscious when serving.
- Overthinking technique after making mistakes.
- Feeling overwhelmed by conflicting advice.
- Becoming tense when receiving coaching during matches.
- Comparing progress with players who have previous racket-sport experience.

### Strategies

**1. Separate practice from competition.**  
Practice is where technique is developed. Matches are where existing skills are used.

**2. Limit technical thoughts during games.**  
Choose one simple cue and trust the movement.

**3. Avoid constant technique changes.**  
A few missed shots don't necessarily indicate a mechanical problem.

**4. Manage outside distractions.**  
Avoid processing multiple coaching instructions during a match. Save detailed feedback for practice.

**5. Focus on personal progress.**  
Players with tennis or other racket-sport backgrounds may develop certain skills faster. Improvement should be measured against personal progress, not someone else's experience.

**6. Develop a reset routine.**  
After a mistake, relax the shoulders, exhale, and focus on the next point.

**Key reminder:** One missed shot is not a reason to question the entire technique.
